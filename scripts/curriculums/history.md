# History — the plan

**Status: complete, 2026-11-04. 12 stages, 359 lessons, 1,291 cards, 945
lesson sources, all applied.** Approved the same day with the full length kept.
Stages 2–12 were written in parallel from one brief and merged by a script that
refuses a repeated lesson name or card front across stages, a lesson under
1,200 characters, and any note that reveals a private library. Every primary-
source quotation was fetched from a public-domain text and checked word for
word; anything that could not be fetched is paraphrased and says so.

His rulings: a **world survey**, **from zero**, **chronological with every
region in each age**, **South Africa kept brief** (SA Politics covers it in
depth, and this points across), and **any free resource**, meaning free online or a printed book.

## Goal

Know the shape of the human past well enough to place anything in it: what
came before what, what was happening elsewhere at the same time, and why the
big turns happened. Then go deep enough at each turn to follow an argument
about it.

**Not to arrive at a position**, his standing rule on contested subjects. Where
historians genuinely disagree (why Rome fell, what caused the First World War,
how to count the dead of a famine), the lesson gives the strongest form of each
reading and says what evidence would settle it.

## How it is read

Every stage is **one age, seen across the world**: what Rome, Han China, the
Mauryas and Meroë were each doing in the same centuries, not Europe with
side-trips. Each stage opens with a **map lesson** (where everyone is) and
closes with a **connections lesson** (who traded with, fought, or converted
whom). A lesson teaches its point in full. Sources are attribution and further
reading, never where the substance lives.

Each stage also has **one primary-source lesson**: a short, free, public-domain
passage read closely (Herodotus on the Persian Wars, the Analects, Bede, Ibn
Battuta, a Lincoln speech), so that history is met as evidence and not only as
narrative.

## The resources

**Free online:**
- **OpenStax *World History***, Vol. 1 (to 1500) and Vol. 2 (from 1400). Its
  chronological, all-regions order is the order this follows, and its chapters
  are the further reading for each stage. **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and OpenStax asks
  that it not be used with AI without permission**, so it is read and cited,
  never adapted. Lessons are written fresh.
- **Internet History Sourcebooks** (Fordham): the primary-source passages for
  each stage.
- **Project Gutenberg / Internet Archive**: public-domain translations of
  Herodotus, Thucydides, Polybius, Tacitus, Suetonius, Procopius, Bede, the
  Analects (Legge), the Arthashastra (Shamasastry), Marco Polo, and others.
- **Khan Academy World History** and **Crash Course World History**: free
  overviews, for further watching.
- **Our World in Data** (CC BY): population, life expectancy and wealth over
  time, for the modern stages' numbers.

**Printed books** (depth and further reading):
- *Ancient Near East:* Kuhrt's *The Persian Empire* (sources) and *Hellenism in
  the East*; Dalley's *The City of Babylon*; Toro's *Pax Assyriaca*.
- *Rome and Byzantium:* Gibbon's *Decline and Fall*; Kaldellis's *The New Roman
  Empire* and *Romanland*; Treadgold's *History of the Byzantine State*;
  Norwich's *Short History of Byzantium*; Harl on Alexander; Fustel de
  Coulanges's *The Ancient City*; Eusebius.
- *The wider world:* Fisher's *History of India*; Man's *The Mongol Empire*;
  the Cambridge History of Japan and Gordon's *Modern History of Japan*;
  Barnhart's *Ancient Civilizations of North America*; Wheatcroft's *Infidels*;
  Manchester's *A World Lit Only by Fire*.
- *The modern world:* Churchill's *History of the English-Speaking Peoples* and
  *The Gathering Storm*; Hochschild's *To End All Wars*; Keegan's *American
  Civil War*; Applebaum's *Gulag*; Schlögel's *The Soviet Century*;
  Feinstein's economic history and the Cambridge History of South Africa (for
  the brief South African thread).

## How a lesson is written

- **Teaches, not points.** Who, where, when, what happened, why it mattered,
  each in full, in plain prose.
- **Dates and a place in every lesson**, so the cards have something to hold.
- **Contested points are marked as contested**: the strongest readings, and
  what would decide between them.
- **Further reading per lesson**: the OpenStax chapter, the primary source, and
  a printed book for depth.

## Cards

`word` cards for people, places, terms and dates ("Battle of Talas — 751, Tang
v. Abbasids"); `grammar` cards for "why did…" questions with a worked answer.
Roughly 3 to 4 per lesson, unlocked as each lesson is ticked.
