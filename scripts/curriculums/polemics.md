# Polemics — the plan

**Status: complete, 2026-11-04. 13 stages, 287 lessons, 814 cards, 616 lesson
sources, all applied.** Approved the same day with every open question agreed.
Written by four writers (Stages 1–4, 5–7, 8–10, 11–13) and merged by the same
checks as History, Philosophy and Theology. Every quotation was fetched from a
public-domain or freely licensed text and checked word for word.

His rulings:
- **Its own curriculum**, split out of Theology ("No polemics will be its own
  curriculum"). Theology teaches the faith from the inside; this answers what
  stands against it or beside it.
- **Everything answered**: Roman Catholicism, Protestantism, the New Age,
  atheism, Buddhism, and the other religions.
- **Pan-Orthodox, no writer excluded**, as in Theology.
- **Any free resource**, meaning free online or a printed book.

## Goal

Know every major alternative to the Orthodox faith well enough that its own
best defender would say "yes, that is what we believe", know the Orthodox
answer to it and where that answer comes from, and be able to give that answer
in conversation, with charity.

## The method, which every lesson keeps

Polemics answers; it does not stay neutral. That is the difference from
History and Philosophy, where the rule is **not to arrive at a position**. So
the rule here keeps the fairness and changes the ending:

1. **Their case at its strongest**, from their own texts and their best
   defenders: the Catechism of the Catholic Church, the Book of Concord, the
   Qur'an, the Pali Canon, *The God Delusion*. Never from an exposé or a
   hostile summary: a position learned in a caricature is answered wrongly,
   and the person across the table notices.
2. **The Orthodox answer**, from Scripture, the councils, the Fathers and the
   modern Orthodox writers, saying which.
3. **Their best reply** to that answer, where there is one.
4. **What it finally rests on**: the one or two premises the whole
   disagreement turns on, so that he knows where a conversation will end up.

**Where Orthodox writers answer differently**, both are given at their
strongest, as in Theology. The live ones: how strictly to receive the
heterodox (baptism or chrismation, *akriveia* or *oikonomia*); St Augustine's
standing; evolution and the age of the earth; ecumenism and dialogue; and what
may be hoped for those outside the Church.

**History told honestly.** Where Christians, the Orthodox included, have
wronged the people now being answered (the sack of Constantinople is not the
only page; the Church's own anti-Jewish preaching, forced conversions, the
Uniate quarrels from both sides), the lesson says so plainly. An answer that
hides its own side's history is weaker, not stronger.

**Charity is part of the subject.** The Fathers wrote against heresies, not
against people, and the stage that closes the curriculum is about the
conversation itself.

## How it is read

- Each stage opens with a **"Context"** lesson and closes with a **"Looking
  back"** lesson, as in the other three.
- Each stage has **two "Reading" lessons**: one passage from the other side's
  own text and one from the Orthodox answer to it, read side by side.
  Quotations come only from public-domain or freely licensed translations,
  fetched and checked word for word. Anything in copyright is paraphrased and
  says so.
- **Present-day facts carry "as of November 2026"**: numbers of adherents,
  the dialogues, which church has recognised what.
- **Theology, Philosophy and History point across**: the councils and the
  Fathers are taught there, the arguments for God in Philosophy, the
  Reformation and Islam's history in History. This curriculum does not
  re-teach them; it uses them.

## The resources

**Free online, their own texts:**
- Rome: the *Catechism of the Catholic Church* and the councils' documents on
  vatican.va; Vatican I's *Pastor Aeternus* and the older papal documents in
  public-domain translations; the joint statements of the Orthodox–Catholic
  dialogue (Ravenna 2007, Chieti 2016, Alexandria 2023).
- The Reformation: the Book of Concord (bookofconcord.org), Luther's works in
  public-domain translations, Calvin's *Institutes* and the Westminster
  Confession on CCEL, the Thirty-Nine Articles.
- The new movements: their own sites and scriptures — jw.org,
  churchofjesuschrist.org and the Book of Mormon, the Adventists' Fundamental
  Beliefs, *Science and Health*.
- Islam: the Qur'an in public-domain and free translations (Pickthall,
  quran.com's parallel texts), the hadith collections on sunnah.com.
- Judaism: the Tanakh (JPS 1917, public domain), Sefaria for the Talmud and
  the commentators.
- India and the East: SuttaCentral's free translations of the Pali Canon
  (Sujato, CC0); the Upanishads and the Gita in public-domain translations;
  the *Tao Te Ching* (Legge).
- The New Age and the occult: Blavatsky, Steiner and the early Theosophists,
  all public domain.
- Atheism: Hume's *Dialogues*, Feuerbach, Nietzsche, Russell's essays; the
  *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* on atheism, the problem of evil, and
  each religion.

**Free online, the Orthodox answer:**
- The Fathers on CCEL and New Advent: St Irenaeus *Against Heresies*, St
  Justin's *Dialogue with Trypho*, St Athanasius *Against the Heathen*, St
  John of Damascus *On Heresies* (with chapter 100 on Islam).
- The Orthodox statements: St Mark of Ephesus's encyclical; the Confession of
  Dositheus (1672); the Encyclical of 1848; Patriarch Anthimos's reply to Leo
  XIII (1895); the 1920 Encyclical; the Greek Church on Freemasonry (1933).
- St Gregory Palamas's letter from captivity among the Turks; Manuel II's
  dialogue with a Persian.
- Fr John Romanides's essays (romanity.org); oca.org's questions and answers.

**Printed books** (depth): Fr Seraphim Rose, *Orthodoxy and the Religion of
the Future*, *Nihilism*, *Genesis, Creation, and Early Man*; Met. Hierotheos
Vlachos, *The Difference between Orthodox Spirituality and Other Traditions*;
Kalomiros, *Against False Union*; St Justin Popović, *The Orthodox Church and
Ecumenism*; Fr Peter Heers on the baptism of the heterodox; Siecienski,
*Beards, Azymes, and Purgatory*; Pelikan, *The Christian Tradition*; Yannaras,
*Orthodoxy and the West*; Ware, *The Orthodox Church*; De Young, *Religion of
the Apostles*; Voorhis's translation of St John of Damascus on Islam;
Wheatcroft, *Infidels*. On the other side, read as their best defenders:
Dawkins, *The God Delusion*; Russell, *Why I Am Not a Christian*; Oppy's
*Companion to Atheism and Philosophy*; Bhikkhu Bodhi, *In the Buddha's Words*;
Nibley on the Book of Mormon; Chryssides on the Witnesses. And the answers to
atheism: Hart, *Atheist Delusions*; McGrath, *The Dawkins Delusion*; Nagel,
*Mind and Cosmos*; van Inwagen, *The Problem of Evil*; Keener,
*Christobiography*; Feser.

## Cards

`word` cards for terms (with the Greek or Arabic or Pali where it matters),
people with their dates, councils and documents with what they said;
`grammar` cards in three shapes: "what do they hold about…", "how does the
Church answer…", and "what does it finally rest on…". About 3 to 4 per lesson.
