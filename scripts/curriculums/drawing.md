# Drawing — Learning Path

## Goal

Draw what is in front of you, and then draw what is not. Figures and heads
first, because those are the hardest and the most useful; perspective and
composition because a figure has to stand somewhere.

The practical end of it is Shepherds. Timon has final say on design and draws
by hand, so this is about being able to contribute and to understand what he
is doing — not about replacing him.

## Where this came from

Designed, not transcribed. There is no drawing curriculum on the machine, but
there is a complete classical library, and this path is built around what is
actually there rather than around books that would have to be bought:

| Stage | Books on hand |
| --- | --- |
| Seeing | Betty Edwards *Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain*; Dodson *Keys to Drawing* |
| Gesture | Nicolaides *The Natural Way to Draw*; Vilppu *Drawing Manual*, *Drawing Animals* |
| Construction | Vilppu *Figure Drawing*; Loomis *Figure Drawing For All It's Worth* |
| Perspective | D'Amelio *Perspective Drawing Handbook*; Norling *Perspective Made Easy* |
| Anatomy | Bridgman *Constructive Anatomy*, *The Human Machine*; Hogarth *Dynamic Anatomy*; Goldfinger *Human Anatomy for Artists*; Simblet *Anatomy for the Artist* |
| Head | Loomis *Drawing the Head and Hands*; Hogarth *Drawing the Human Head*; Civardi *Drawing Portraits* |
| Composition | Dow *Composition*; Guptill *Sketching and Rendering in Pencil*; Loomis *Creative Illustration* |
| Colour | Edwards *Color* |

## Why the milestones have numbers on them

Drawing is the one subject here where the quantity *is* the method. "Understand
gesture" is not a milestone; **100 thirty-second gestures** is. Nobody has ever
read their way to a good line.

So most entries below are counts. They are deliberately large and deliberately
boring, and finishing one means finishing it — fifty blocked-in figures is not
forty.

## What is thin, and worth knowing

Two real gaps in the library, neither worth buying around yet:

- **Colour and light.** Only Edwards' *Color*, which is a colour-theory course
  rather than a book about light. Gurney's *Color and Light* is the standard
  and is not here.
- **Constructed perspective.** D'Amelio and Norling are both solid and both
  old-fashioned; Scott Robertson's *How to Draw* is the modern reference for
  drawing invented objects accurately, and is not here either.

Neither blocks anything before stage 7.

## The stages

1. **Seeing** — unlearning the symbol for a thing and drawing the thing.
   Edwards' course is built for exactly this and is where to start, including
   the pre-instruction drawings, which exist to be compared against in stage 8.
2. **Gesture** — the line of action before the anatomy. Done first on purpose:
   a figure built on correct muscles and no gesture is a diagram.
3. **Form and construction** — spheres, boxes, cylinders, then figures made of
   them. The point at which drawing from imagination becomes possible.
4. **Perspective** — because a figure has to stand somewhere, and a scene in
   Shepherds has to have a floor.
5. **Anatomy** — after construction, never before. Anatomy learned without
   construction is a vocabulary with no grammar.
6. **Head and portrait** — the hardest single thing, and the one a viewer
   judges hardest, because everyone is an expert on faces.
7. **Composition and rendering** — making a picture rather than a study.
8. **Keeping it** — volume, forever, plus one honest comparison against the
   drawings from stage 1.
