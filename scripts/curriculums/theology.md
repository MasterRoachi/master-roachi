# Theology — the plan

**Status: complete, 2026-11-04. 12 stages, 318 lessons, 979 cards, 708 lesson
sources, all applied.** Approved the same day: the Oriental Orthodox in Stage 7
with the dialogues in Stage 12, the calendar and the Old Calendarists given
fairly, the full length kept. Written by four writers, three stages each, and
merged by the same checks as History and Philosophy. Every quotation was fetched
from a public-domain text and checked word for word.

His rulings:
- **Orthodox theology only**, but **pan-Orthodox**: Constantinople, Alexandria,
  Antioch, Jerusalem, Russia, Serbia, Romania, Bulgaria, Georgia, Cyprus, Greece,
  Poland, Albania, the Czech lands and Slovakia, the OCA, and the churches of the
  diaspora.
- **No writer is excluded.**
- **Any free resource**, meaning free online or a printed book.
- **No polemics here.** Answering Rome, the Reformation, the New Age, atheism,
  Buddhism and the rest is its own curriculum, planned after this one.

## Goal

Know what the Orthodox Church believes, prays and has lived, from the inside,
well enough to explain any part of it and say where it comes from: Scripture,
the councils, the Fathers, the services.

**Taught as the Church teaches it.** This is theology, not religious studies.
Where Orthodox writers genuinely differ — between jurisdictions, schools or
centuries — the lesson says so and gives each view in its strongest form. Where
the Church has spoken through a council, it says that plainly.

The Philosophy curriculum covers the same thinkers as arguments (Augustine,
Palamas, Maximus). This one covers them as the Church's teachers, and the two
point across to each other.

## How it is read

- Each stage opens with a **"Context"** lesson and closes with a **"Looking
  back"** lesson.
- Each stage has **one "Reading" lesson**: a passage from Scripture, a Father or
  a service text, read closely. Quotations come only from public-domain
  translations fetched and checked word for word (the Ante-Nicene and Nicene
  Fathers, older Scripture translations, older service books). Anything in
  copyright is paraphrased and says so.
- **Greek and Slavonic terms** are given with their meaning the first time they
  appear (theosis, hypostasis, ousia, energeia, nous, the Theotokos, the
  Typikon), and they become cards. The Greek curriculum points across.

## The resources

**Free online:**
- Fr Thomas Hopko, *The Orthodox Faith*, four volumes (oca.org). The OCA's own
  catechism, and the backbone of Stages 2–6.
- The Ante-Nicene and Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers (public domain, on CCEL
  and New Advent).
- The OCA's lives of the saints and feasts; the Greek Archdiocese's online
  chapel; the Antiochian Archdiocese's resources; the service texts of the
  Liturgy of St John Chrysostom in public-domain translations.
- *The Philokalia*, and older public-domain translations of parts of it.

**Printed books** (depth):
- Introductions: Ware, *The Orthodox Church* and *The Orthodox Way*; Lossky,
  *The Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church*; Alfeyev's three volumes;
  Yannaras, *Elements of Faith*; Hopko; McGuckin, *The Orthodox Church*.
- History and theology of the councils: Behr, *The Formation of Christian
  Theology*; Meyendorff, *Byzantine Theology*; Davis, *The First Seven Ecumenical
  Councils*; Louth on St John of Damascus.
- The Fathers: Irenaeus, Athanasius, Chrysostom, Cyril of Alexandria.
- The spiritual life: the Philokalia (five volumes), *Unseen Warfare*,
  St John of Kronstadt, St Sophrony.
- Worship and Scripture: Sokolov on the services, the Orthodox Study Bible,
  Archbishop Averky on the Gospels.
- Modern writers: Fr Seraphim Rose, St Justin Popović, Schmemann, Florovsky.

## Cards

`word` cards for terms (with the Greek), people with their dates, councils with
what they decided, and feasts with their dates. `grammar` cards for "what does
the Church teach about…" and "why…", with the answer set out. About 3 to 4 per
lesson.

## How it will be written

With **four writers rather than twelve**, his ruling on usage: Stages 1–3, 4–6,
7–9 and 10–12, each writer taking three stages in order. The merge applies the
same checks as History and Philosophy.
