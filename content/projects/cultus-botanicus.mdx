You buy an abandoned rural nursery on the word of an old man who calls himself
the Teller of Tales. What comes with it is a neglected property, a little
equipment, a handful of plants, and one usable corner of greenhouse.

Everything after that is breeding.

## 961 species, and none of them written by hand

Thirty-one foliage types and thirty-one flower types cross into 961 core
species. The system that decides what comes of a given pairing is generated by
a rule rather than authored as a table — which means it is consistent
everywhere, it can be checked, and it is genuinely learnable rather than
memorisable.

That last part is the whole design. The intended arc is from

> *I wonder what happens if I cross these*

to

> *I know exactly what I need to breed next*

and the mastery has to come from understanding the plants, not from a
character sheet quietly getting better numbers.

## The loop

Grow, breed, discover, preserve or sell, upgrade, breed deeper. Catch insects,
keep back useful stock, restore more of the greenhouse than you started with.
Every other system exists to support, complicate or extend the breeding.

## The Twelve

Old botanical records and Terrath folklore describe twelve extraordinary
plants. Modern botanists in the setting dispute that they were ever real.

Recreating all twelve is the game.

They are not a secret 962nd species. Each is one of the 961 grown into a
condition nobody has reached — a state, not a recipe — which is why they can be
specified against the trait system rather than hidden behind a lookup nobody
could deduce.

## Where it stands

Both tracks are built and playable: the breeding prototype and the rendering
spike, with 200 headless checks passing. Godot 4, with Python tools that prove
the breeding rule's invariants and back the trait numbers — the tools gate the
implementation rather than describing it after the fact.

It sits in [Terrath](/projects/terrath/), roughly 165 years before
*Shepherds We Shall Be*, in the decades before the technological acceleration.
