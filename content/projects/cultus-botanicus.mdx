Inspired by *Plant Tycoon*, the 2007 nursery sim, and made to go further than
it did: deeper breeding, upgrades worth saving for, and a world of its own.

The bones are the same, because the bones were good. You run a nursery. You
grow things, cross-breed them, sell what you can bear to part with and keep
what you cannot, upgrade the greenhouse a corner at a time, and chase insects
around it with a net.

You get there on the word of an old man who calls himself the Teller of Tales,
and what he sells you is a neglected property, a little equipment, a handful of
plants and one usable corner of glass.

## 961 species

<Split src="/projects/cultus-botanicus-a.webp" side="left">

Thirty-one foliage types and thirty-one flower types cross into 961 species.
Nothing about which pairing gives what is written by hand — it comes out of a
rule, which means it is consistent everywhere and can actually be *learned*
rather than looked up.

That is the whole design, really. The arc is from *I wonder what happens if I
cross these* to *I know exactly what I need to breed next*, and the mastery has
to sit in your head rather than in a character sheet quietly gaining numbers.

</Split>

## The Twelve

Old records and Terrath folklore describe twelve extraordinary plants. Botanists
in the setting say they were never real.

<Split src="/projects/cultus-botanicus-b.webp" side="right">

Growing all twelve is the game. They are not a hidden 962nd species — each one
is an ordinary member of the 961 grown into a condition nobody has managed
before. A state, not a recipe. Which means you can work out what one needs
instead of waiting for someone to publish the answer.

</Split>

## Where it is

It is resting for now. The breeding underneath works; the nursery wrapped
around it is not yet the place it should be, and it will wait until it can
be.

It sits in [Terrath](/projects/terrath/) about 165 years before
[Shepherds We Shall Be](/projects/shepherds-we-shall-be/) — the same world, a
couple of generations earlier, while things were still quiet.
