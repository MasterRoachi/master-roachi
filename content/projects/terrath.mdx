Terrath is a world, and it is the reason there are two games rather than one
game with a sequel.

[Shepherds We Shall Be](/projects/shepherds-we-shall-be/) and
[Cultus Botanicus](/projects/cultus-botanicus/) both happen here, about a
century and a half apart, and neither of them owns the place. A game will bend
a world to suit a mechanic if you let it. This one gets to say no.

## Three realms, stacked

<Split src="/projects/terrath-a.webp" side="right">

There is **Ourean** above — the radiant unseen realm, where the Creator and the
beings made before the world belong. There is Terrath in the middle, which is
where people live. And there is **Gehenna** below, dark and layered, with a
floor to it: the Realm of Ash and Darkness, the furthest point in creation from
the light at the top.

They sit on one axis. Which end of it a thing is nearer to is not a metaphor.

</Split>

## It was sung

The world was given form through a First Song, which has a consequence people
tend to skip past: in a world that was sung, the practitioners are **singers**.
The Cantors are an ancient tradition of trained voice and hard discipline, and
what they are doing is not decoration. They are working on the same structure
underneath everything that a much later civilisation would learn to reach with
machines instead.

That structure has a name — Resonance — and a corruption, Dissonance. A person
can be damaged at that level in ways that are not death and not madness, and
the world has words for those too.

## Who lives there

Fifteen peoples so far, and they are not variations on humans with different
hats.

**Boulderkin** are stone and flesh, mountain refuges and durable craft.
**Coral Singers** are human-descended and aquatic — gills, webbed hands,
harmonic song, and a long quarrel with things in the sea that went wrong.
**Emberkin** walk out volcanic country as nomadic smiths and hold the Auric
Phoenix in reverence. **Luminarians** came out of the ancient war attuned to
what was left of the Creator's echoes, faintly bioluminescent, keeping their
history by mouth and their medicine in chants.

And AGI robots are filed as a people, alongside the rest. That is a position,
not a technicality.

Twenty-nine creatures are written down with them — an obsidian leviathan, a
drowned colossus, a warden of chains, a fallow broodmother — and forty-odd
places, from Caereth Vahl to Cold Harbor to the Elderstone Shrine.

## The rule everything is argued against

Magic and technology are not two orders of reality. They are two ways of
reaching the same one, so the apparatus never settles the moral question.

The distinction is **stewardship against domination** — tending something you
were entrusted with and did not make, against mastering it because you are
strong enough to. Both are available to a priest and to an engineer.

And the part that does the most work: not knowing the structure is real does
not make it safe to pull on.
