Terrath is the world. *Shepherds We Shall Be* and
[Cultus Botanicus](/projects/cultus-botanicus/) are both set in it, roughly a
century and a half apart, and neither of them owns it.

That separation is the point. A game will always bend the world to suit a
mechanic if you let it. Here the world is its own repository with its own
authority, and a product can *propose* a fact but cannot promote one by itself.

## What is in it

Close to two hundred canon records, sorted by what they are rather than where
they turned up: locations, characters, creatures, peoples, history, factions,
institutions, species, cultures, artifacts, technology, metaphysics, cosmology,
and the realms themselves.

The largest sections are the ones a story actually walks through — forty-odd
places and thirty-odd people.

## Written down so it can be checked

Every record carries its status, its scope, where the fact came from, and what
it was derived from. Locked canon can only change through an accepted decision
record. A validator reads the whole estate — canon, decisions, registries,
sources, chronology, terminology, aliases, open questions — and fails on a
contradiction rather than letting one sit.

Legacy wording is treated as evidence, not authority. Where two sources
disagree the disagreement gets surfaced, not silently resolved in favour of
whichever was read last.

That sounds like bureaucracy for a fictional world. It is the difference
between a setting you can build two games in and a folder of notes that stopped
agreeing with itself years ago.

## The shape of it

Terrath is one realm among several. It was given form through the First Song,
and the metaphysics that follow from that — resonance and its dissonance,
what binding and hollowing do to a person, where stewardship turns into
domination — are canon in their own right rather than magic-system trivia
attached to a game.
