Terrath is a world, and it is the reason there are two games rather than one
game with a sequel.

[Shepherds We Shall Be](/projects/shepherds-we-shall-be/) and
[Cultus Botanicus](/projects/cultus-botanicus/) both happen here, about a
century and a half apart, and neither of them owns the place. A game will bend
a world to suit a mechanic if you let it. This one gets to say no.

## What it is

<Split src="/projects/terrath-a.webp" side="right">

Terrath was sung into being. There is a Creator, called the Eternal Harmony,
and a First Song through which the world was given form — and there are beings
who were made before it, one of whom did not stay.

Underneath everything there is a structure. It can be reached by long practice,
and — much later, by people who do not believe it is there — by machine.

</Split>

## The idea the whole world runs on

Magic and technology are not two orders of reality. They are two ways of
reaching the same one, which means the apparatus never settles the moral
question. Doing a thing with a machine rather than a rite changes the method,
not the nature of what was done.

So the distinction is not ritual against science. It is **stewardship against
domination** — tending something you were entrusted with and did not make,
against mastering it because you are strong enough to. Both are available to a
priest and to an engineer.

And the part that does the most work: not knowing the structure is real does
not make it safe to pull on.

## What happened

There was an ancient war, and a very long time afterwards.

Then there was a modern era — one that got good enough at things to reproduce
effects the old traditions had described, without ever accepting the
explanation that came with them.

Three generations after that, in a small pastoral settlement, a boy grows up
knowing exactly what happened to the world.

He is wrong.
