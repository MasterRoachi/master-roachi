Three generations after a supposed zombie disease ended civilisation, a young
Shepherd named Eli wakes a dormant AI orb and follows a signal that should not
exist. He is not the last of anything. The world moved on, settled into
something smaller, and decided it already knew what had happened to it.

It is a top-down 2D pixel-art action RPG, built solo in Godot with GDScript,
set in [Terrath](/projects/terrath/) — the same world as
[Cultus Botanicus](/projects/cultus-botanicus/), which sits about a century and
a half away from it.

## How it plays

Four heroes are active at once and **all four are directly playable**. You hold
one and switch freely mid-fight; the other three run on authored AI with
priorities and builds you set beforehand, and you push them around with party
commands and player-triggered Unity combinations rather than by micromanaging
them.

The intent is that positioning, defence and timing decide a fight — not input
speed, and not enemies with inflated health bars. A companion is a character
who fights alongside you, not a second cursor.

## Where it actually is

Two tracks, running in parallel and deliberately not blocking each other.

**The engine** has cleared R0, the whole Foundation Lab, and P0 through P2 —
P1 and P2 both approved by playtest on 3 September 2026. P3 is next. Every
milestone has to remain playable before the following one starts, which is
slower than it sounds and the reason there is no date attached to any of this.

**The art** pipeline is accepted end to end: generated page, automatic
conversion, human approval, then into Aseprite. The first asset's Southeast
shamble is approved and the next action page is the open item. The engine work
runs on placeholder art until real sprites arrive, so neither track waits on
the other.

## The part worth admitting

In August 2026 the combat prototype and the A.P.E.-3 vertical slice were
**cancelled** — eleven phases of prototype and six of vertical slice, retired
as the production line of work.

Not deleted. They are kept as a reference implementation and a mechanics
laboratory, because what they taught was real and the code still answers
questions. But they were not a foundation, and continuing to build on them
would have meant carrying every early decision forward on the grounds that it
already existed. The campaign, the recruitment order, the locations, the
bosses and the narrative all survived the reboot unchanged. Only the
implementation went.

That is the single most useful thing that has happened to this project, and it
looks like a step backwards on any chart you could draw of it.

## The constraint that shapes everything

Roughly five hours a week, one person, PC only, keyboard and mouse. Progress is
gated on quality rather than dates, and a complete smaller release beats an
unfinished large one every time. Twenty hours of game is a long-term
aspiration, not a commitment anybody has made.
