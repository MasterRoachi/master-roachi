Sprite-acus took a picture of a character and turned it into pixel art that
moves — the frames a game needs to make someone walk, swing a sword, or fall
over.

Making the picture was the easy half. The half that used to eat an evening was
everything after it: cutting the picture into frames, making every frame the
same size, and getting every colour to match the rest of the game. Sprite-acus
did that part.

<Split src="/projects/art/sprite-acus-sprite.webp" alt="A pixel art gladiator sprite produced by the tool." side="right">

What came back was never quite finished. It always needed a touch-up by hand,
and closing that gap was the next job. It would be dishonest to describe it any
other way.

</Split>

## Where it honestly is

Shelved, and due a rework before it is picked up again.

It was made for [Shepherds We Shall Be](/projects/shepherds-we-shall-be/), and
in September 2026 Shepherds stopped using generated art altogether: its art is drawn
by hand now, with Timon. So the tool lost the thing it was being built for, and
by late October 2026 it was plain it was not working as well as it should. It went
on the shelf rather than on a page pretending otherwise.
