I cannot draw. Sprite-acus is how there are sprites.

The idea is that you generate a picture of any character you can imagine, and a
click or two later you have a high-quality animated pixel art sprite that a
game engine will actually take. No pixel art skill in the middle.

It is for anyone in the same position — generating art and needing it to come
out the other side as a usable game sprite.

## Where it honestly is

Super alpha. It works, and it is what the sprites in
[Shepherds We Shall Be](/projects/shepherds-we-shall-be/) are made with, so it
is doing real work rather than sitting in a repo being a good idea.

But it still hands you something that needs a pass in Aseprite before it is
finished. Closing that gap — getting from generated image to done, without the
manual cleanup — is what is being built next. Until then, "click or two of a
button" is the goal rather than the current state, and it would be dishonest to
describe it any other way.

## What it will not do

It does not make artistic decisions, produce the final master, or import
anything into an engine. A successful conversion means mechanically valid,
never artistically approved — a distinction worth keeping, because the
alternative is a tool that quietly decides things an artist should.

## All or nothing

If one frame of a sixteen-frame sheet fails, nothing appears at the destination
at all, and the failure names the frame and the cell it came from. A
half-published animation is worse than none, because it looks finished.

Everything it writes is then re-derived independently and checked against what
it meant to write.

## Its colours are not its own

The window's surfaces and accents are *found* in whatever master palette the
loaded project points at, using the same nearest-colour search the converter
runs on a sprite. Load the Shepherds palette and the app comes up in Terrath's
colours; load another project's and it wears theirs.

There is deliberately no theme setting. A palette already says what a project's
colours are, and asking it to say so twice is how two copies drift apart.
