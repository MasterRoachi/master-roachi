Sixty-five achievements. Two of them can be permanently lost by killing
somebody you were supposed to leave alive, one of them takes the entire game to
earn, and a handful more are famous for simply not firing.

This is the route, the collectibles, and the parts of the game worth actually
knowing about — the plasmids that carry a run, where the money comes from, and
what research is really for.

## Before you start

**Difficulty.** Everything can be done in one Survivor run. Survivor unlocks
*A Man Chooses* and, with Vita-Chambers off, *I Chose the Impossible* — and
because the Hard achievements sit below them, you get *Seriously Good At This*
and *Brass Balls* on the way past. One run, four difficulty achievements.

**Vita-Chambers.** Turn them off in the options before you begin. *Brass Balls*
and *I Chose the Impossible* require finishing without using one, and the
reliable way to not use one is to make it impossible. Dying then sends you to
your last save, so save often and save deliberately.

**The honest alternative.** If Survivor without respawns is not your idea of a
good time, do a normal run first and use New Game Plus for the difficulty
achievements — you keep your weapons and plasmids, which makes the second pass
much shorter. Slower in total, far less painful.

## The route

Fifteen levels, in this order:

Welcome to Rapture · Medical Pavilion · Neptune's Bounty · Smuggler's Hideout ·
Arcadia · Farmer's Market · Fort Frolic · Hephaestus · Rapture Central Control ·
Olympus Heights · Apollo Square · Point Prometheus · Proving Grounds ·
Fontaine's Lair

<Split src="/writing/bioshock/rapture.webp" alt="The Welcome to Rapture entrance hall." side="right">

There is no level select and no chapter replay that keeps your progress. Once a
level is behind you, what you left there is gone for that run — which is what
makes the two missables below permanent rather than annoying.

</Split>

## The two you can genuinely lose

Both are Sander Cohen, and both come down to one decision in **Fort Frolic**.

Cohen appears at the end of his masterpiece and gives you the choice of killing
him or walking away. **Walk away.**

- **Irony** — photograph Cohen's corpse. You come back for this much later, in
  Mercury Suites, and kill him then. Kill him in Fort Frolic and there is no
  corpse to photograph where the achievement expects one.
- **Found Cohen's Room** — his apartment in Mercury Suites only opens because
  Cohen is alive to open it. Kill him early and the door stays shut for the
  rest of the game.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not kill Sander Cohen in Fort Frolic">

One decision, two achievements, no way back. There is no level select, so by
the time you find out, the level is behind you and the run is over.

</Callout>

## The 122 audio diaries

*Historian* wants all 122, and missing exactly one voids it. It is the longest
achievement in the game and the one most likely to end a 100% run at the credits.

The bulk of them sit in the early and middle levels — Medical Pavilion has 17,
Neptune's Bounty 19, Arcadia 17, Fort Frolic 15, Hephaestus 17. That is 85 of
the 122 in five levels, so the discipline you need is front-loaded.

Two habits make this survivable:

1. **Check your count before you take a bathysphere.** The diaries screen shows
   how many you have. Compare it against a per-level list *while you can still
   go back*, not afterwards.
2. **Search every corpse, desk and safe.** Diaries are not marked, do not glow,
   and several sit in rooms you have no other reason to enter.

## Research, and what it is actually for

Twelve of the sixty-five achievements are research. It is also the single most
useful mechanic in the game and most people underuse it.

The camera costs you nothing but film. Photographing an enemy grades the shot,
and enough shots of one type levels up your research on it — each level giving a
permanent damage bonus against that type, and often a free tonic outright.
Research is not a collectible; it is a difficulty setting you control.

**What has to be fully researched:** Thuggish, Leadhead, Nitro, Spider and
Houdini Splicers, Rosies, Bouncers, and Little Sisters.

**Research PhD** goes further and wants everything maxed — including Security
Cameras, Security Bots and Turrets, which people forget are researchable at all.

**Prolific Photographer** wants at least one photo in every research group.
This is the one to keep in mind early, because some subjects appear in a narrow
window and never again.

<Callout type="tip" title="Photograph everything, the first time you see it">

A creature you have not shot yet is a creature you may not get another shot at.
Film is cheap, the research bonuses are permanent, and *Prolific Photographer*
turns on subjects that appear in a narrow window and never again.

</Callout>

## Little Sisters, and why this costs you a run

There are 21. *Little Sister Savior* requires rescuing every one and harvesting
none — a single harvest voids it for that playthrough.

<Split src="/writing/bioshock/big-daddy.webp" alt="A Big Daddy walking with a Little Sister." side="left">

The endings follow the same choice, so you cannot see the harvest ending and
keep *Little Sister Savior* in the same run. Rescue everyone, take the good
ending, and watch the other on a second pass or on YouTube. Rescuing is also
mechanically better: you get ADAM back in gifts, plus tonics and, eventually,
the Big Daddy plasmid.

</Split>

## Tonics, plasmids and the money

**Tonic Collector** wants 53 tonics across Physical, Engineering and Combat.
Most are found or given as research rewards; three must be built at a U-Invent
station, so keep an eye on your components rather than selling everything.

**Weapon Specialist** wants every upgrade on every weapon, which means finding
all **12 Power to the People** stations. Each gives exactly one upgrade and
cannot be reused, so with twelve stations and twelve upgrades there is no slack
— take every one, and do not spend one on a weapon you have already finished.

**The economy.** Money in Rapture comes from three places, in this order of
reliability:

- **Hacking vending machines.** A hacked machine sells cheaper *and* stocks
  things it otherwise would not. Hack them before you buy, not after.
- **Searching everything.** Corpses, bins, desks, cabinets. It is tedious and it
  is most of your income.
- **Rescuing Little Sisters.** The gifts that follow are worth more than the
  ADAM difference against harvesting once you count the tonics.

Hacking is worth doing on principle: seven achievements come from it, including
one for fifty successful hacks, and specific ones for a bot, a turret, a camera,
a vending machine and a safe. The Engineering tonics that restore health and EVE
on a hack turn it into a way of topping up rather than a chore.

<Split src="/writing/bioshock/gatherers-garden.webp" alt="A Gatherer's Garden vending machine." side="right">

**Plasmids worth the ADAM.** *Electro Bolt* is the one you cannot do without —
it stuns almost anything organic, wrecks machines, and shocks anyone standing in
water. Pair it with a wrench and half the game gets easier. *Telekinesis* turns
the scenery into free ammunition and returns grenades to sender. *Security
Bullseye* makes the level fight for you.

</Split>

Buy slots as well as powers. An unslotted plasmid is ADAM you have spent on
nothing.

## The ones that break

This needs saying, because it is not your fault when it happens.

<Callout type="note" title="Some of these are known to be broken">

The remaster has a real reputation for achievements that do not fire —
*Historian*, *Brass Balls*, *Seriously Good At This* and the photography ones
have all been reported as glitched or temperamental. If you have done the work
and nothing pops, you are not going mad.

</Callout>

There are Steam guides hosting **save files** positioned for the missable and
collectible ones. I would rather earn them, and I think you would too — but a
run lost to a bug is not a run you failed, and using a save to recover from one
is a different thing from using it to skip the game. If you go that way:

- Saves live in `Documents\BioshockHD\BioShock\SaveGames`.
- **Back up your own saves first.**
- Disable Vita-Chambers before loading any difficulty save.
- Collectible achievements have been reported not to unlock for some people even
  with the saves, so it is not a guaranteed fix either.

## The short version

1. Survivor, Vita-Chambers off, save constantly.
2. Photograph every new thing the first time you see it.
3. Check your diary count before every bathysphere.
4. Rescue every Little Sister.
5. **Do not kill Sander Cohen in Fort Frolic.**

The fifth one is the only mistake on this list you cannot fix.
