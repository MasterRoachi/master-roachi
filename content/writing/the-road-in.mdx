I was born into the Dutch Reformed church and did not care about it until high
school, at which point I cared about almost everything else instead — New Age,
psychedelics, and eventually Thailand, where I was Buddhist for a while and
meditated for a good deal longer than that.

Then back to Christianity, as a Reformed Baptist. Then I studied. I became
Orthodox in 2021 and was baptised in 2023.

That is the route. What follows is why.

## The questions that would not leave

Four of them, and they gave way in this order.

**Sola Scriptura.** Scripture alone — except the book does not say that about
itself, does not tell you which books it contains, and has to be interpreted by
somebody. "No authority but the text" turns out to mean "no authority but
whoever is reading it", and that is not the same claim.

**Sola Fide.** Faith alone, which requires reading James against himself and a
good deal of the rest against the plain sense.

**The Real Presence.** I could not get the sixth chapter of John to mean what I
needed it to mean. Everyone left over that saying. He let them go and did not
call them back to explain the metaphor.

**The Saints, and the Theotokos in particular.** This was the last one and the
hardest, because it is the one everything in my upbringing was armoured against.

Each of these I first tried to answer from where I already stood. That is what
studying was for. The answers were nearer to hand, and each of them was thinner
than the question.

## What held

Not an argument about any single doctrine in the end. It was the thing sitting
underneath all four.

Tradition is necessary, and so is a normative authority for interpreting it and
for governing the Church. Without those you get exactly what we have — a
thousand sincere readers with a thousand readings and no way to settle any of
it. And once you accept that such an authority is necessary, you have to ask
where it actually is. That question has one answer, and the answer is apostolic
succession.

I did not want it to be. I checked, at length, hoping to find that it was not.

## What it costs

Everything, more or less.

I spent six years at university training to be a pastor and walked out of it
into a vocation I can no longer hold, because in Orthodoxy a man does not simply
decide to be a priest. There are men in this country leading enormous churches
with the same qualification I have — in some cases less of it — and I could have
had that. It was genuinely available. Choosing what is true is expensive, and
anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

I abandoned the faith of my ancestors, which is not a small thing to do to a
family. Most people I know are now fairly certain I have joined a cult.

But I found Christ. So what does any of that matter. A man finds treasure buried
in a field, and he goes and sells everything he has, and he buys the field, and
nobody watching thinks he has got a bargain.
